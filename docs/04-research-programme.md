# Four questions, in dependency order

*Ferney Bernal Buitrago · September 2026 · CC BY 4.0*

*Status: a sketch of what it would take to answer the question in [01-the-question](01-the-question.md). No commitment. Offered so that anyone who wants to pick up any part of it can, with or without me.*

Each is answerable on its own, produces a publishable result on its own, and can be killed by a specific finding — stated under each.

## Study 1 — How did Australia end up with five sets of referral criteria and no measure of adherence to any of them?

A policy analysis. Map each jurisdiction's criteria programme — Queensland's 419 criteria across 27 specialties, Victoria's, NSW's expanding set, WA's eight mandatory specialties and their October 2026 reversion to advisory, SA's — against the absence of any adherence measure in national, state, accreditation, PHN or clinical-indicator frameworks. Set it against the international picture: England pays on one item; New Zealand does not collate declines; nobody publishes.

- Needs: nothing not already held. No data access, no ethics approval, no funding.
- Time: 3–6 months. Output: an analysis piece for an Australian general-practice or medical journal.
- Killed by: an accrediting body confirming it holds aggregate GP2.3 compliance data, or a state confirming a recurring internal adherence report. Close that first.

## Study 2 — Can referral content be scored reliably, and does an automated scorer agree with clinicians?

The instrument-validation study. A retrospective, de-identified corpus of referrals at one health service. A sample scored by two or more blinded clinician raters against the relevant criteria, with inter-rater agreement reported. The same sample scored automatically. Agreement between human and machine reported by specialty and by criterion. A 2026 review found most NLP-on-referrals work still at prototype stage and only a minority evaluated prospectively, so a well-designed validation study is a contribution rather than a repeat.

- Needs: human research ethics approval, a data custodian, a corpus, a collaborator who has run a study before.
- Time: 12–18 months. Output: a methods paper and the instrument.
- Killed by: the human raters failing to agree with each other. If two experienced clinicians cannot agree what a complete referral looks like, the construct is not measurable — which is itself worth publishing.

## Study 3 — Did introducing statewide criteria actually change what referrals contain?

Australia ran an unplanned natural experiment: Queensland's criteria went live progressively from 2015, Victoria's and NSW's later, WA's are being walked back in 2026. Staggered introduction across jurisdictions is the design an interrupted time series or difference-in-differences wants. The only outcome figure anywhere — Queensland's return rate falling from 3.75% to under 0.5% — appeared once, in an audit report, with no source or method, and is equally consistent with services having stopped returning referrals. A health system built the intervention, mandated it, and never evaluated it.

- Needs: multi-site data, funding, a statistician involved before data collection.
- Time: 2–3 years. Output: a policy-relevant evaluation.
- Killed by: no retrievable pre-2015 referral corpus. If historical referrals were faxed and never digitised, there is no "before". Establish this before writing any grant.

## Study 4 — Does referral content quality affect what happens to the patient?

The question that justifies the others, and the largest real gap: no Australian study links referral content quality to clinical outcomes. Link scored content to triage category, time to first appointment, whether the appointment achieved anything, did-not-attend, and — where linkage allows — downstream outcomes.

- Needs: data linkage, competitive funding, a multi-institution team. Not something a practising GP does alone.
- Time: 3–5 years. Output: the evidence the field lacks.
- Killed by: finding no association — which would be the most important result of all, because it would tell four state health departments that their criteria programmes have no measurable patient benefit.

## What any of this needs that I don't currently have

An academic affiliation (for ethics sponsorship and grant eligibility); a senior collaborator who has run a study; a statistician early; and time budgeted for ethics and data governance, because referral letters are among the most sensitive clinical documents there are.

## Why now rather than later

Two systematic reviews landed in this seam within a month of each other in 2026 — a proposed quality framework in the *BJGP* (2026;76(768)) and an NLP review in *Frontiers in Health Services* in April. That is what a field looks like just before it gets busy. The measurement gap has been open for at least fifteen years; it is unlikely to stay open for another five.

Before you continue

What this is

A small, open-source demonstration built to prompt discussion about one question: does a GP referral carry the information the receiving clinician says they need? It shows a fictional referral letter and counts which items from a published referral-criteria list are mentioned in it.

What this is not

  • It is not a clinical tool and is not intended for use in the care of any person.
  • It is not a medical device and makes no clinical recommendation. It does not tell anyone what to order, diagnose or do.
  • It covers one condition, one jurisdiction, one fictional case. Nothing in it has been validated.
  • Do not enter real patient information. Nothing on this page is stored or sent anywhere, and it is not designed to hold patient information.

Referral Completeness Demonstration
A discussion piece about what referrals carry — not a clinical tool

Purpose

Every Australian state publishes lists of what a specialist referral should contain. As far as I could find, no health system routinely measures whether referrals actually contain it. This page lets you see, for one fictional case, what such a check looks like — so that clinicians, patients and people who build health software can argue about whether it is useful, how it should work, and what it gets wrong.

How it works

  • You are shown a fictional referral for adult chest pain.
  • You tick which items the letter contains. The page compares your ticks against the published NSW Health referral criteria for that condition and reports what is mentioned and what is not.
  • That is all it does. The rule list is visible on screen and in the source code, and is reproduced from the NSW Health page it links to.

Limitations, stated up front

  • One condition (adult chest pain), one jurisdiction (NSW), one fictional letter. Other states' criteria are Crown copyright and are deliberately not reproduced here.
  • It counts whether an item is mentioned, not whether it is correct, complete or clinically appropriate. A ticked box is not a clinical judgement.
  • It has not been tested against real referrals and has no evidence of benefit.
  • The published criteria change. This page reproduces the list as it stood on the date shown; the linked NSW page is authoritative.
